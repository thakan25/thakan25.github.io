I'm someone who stops at red lights on an empty road at night. I believe in rules. So when I first read that West Bengal's voter-list revision, the SIR, had struck off roughly **1 in 8 voters**, I didn't believe it could all be legitimate. I went through the data for all 294 seats to find out. Here is what I found, kept short.

## What happened to voters

Between November 2025 and April 2026, the Election Commission's Special Intensive Revision removed **90.8 lakh names (11.9%)** from Bengal's roll of 7.66 crore.

- **58 lakh** were dropped because their form didn't come back: dead, shifted, absent or duplicate. They got **no notice and no hearing**.
- **60 lakh** more were flagged by software, either for having no link to the 2002 list or for a "logical discrepancy" in family details. They had to prove their eligibility with documents. Judges ruled **27 lakh** of them not eligible.

## Where the bias is

I expected bias everywhere. It isn't everywhere, which makes where it *is* more striking.

- **Routine removals were not skewed.** Dead and shifted voters don't cluster in Muslim seats.
- **Flagging was.** In seats under 10% Muslim, 3% of voters were flagged. In Muslim-majority seats, **21%** were: 7 times as many.
- **Judges didn't add the skew.** They actually removed a *smaller* share of flagged voters in Muslim seats (38% vs 62%).

Still, **70%** of the 27 lakh removed after hearings were Muslim, in a state that is about a quarter Muslim. In Nandigram, it was 94.5%, in a seat that is 23% Muslim. And Nandigram isn't alone: there are 37 such seats.

<figure>

<figcaption>The skew enters at flagging, not at the hearing. Source: Sabar Institute seat data.</figcaption>
</figure>

What exactly were those "logical discrepancy" checks, and why did they catch so many more people in Muslim areas? Nobody has explained.

## The remedy that hasn't come

Removed voters can appeal to 19 tribunals. About **38 lakh** appeals were filed. By mid-September only about **3%** had been decided. When they are heard, most voters win: between **8 and 9 in 10** decided appeals put the voter back on the list.

But under a Supreme Court order of 13 April, **you can't vote while your appeal is pending**. The April election went ahead with almost all of them waiting. In July, a Calcutta High Court bench [remarked](https://scroll.in/latest/1094149/bengal-sir-tribunals-could-take-21-years-to-clear-appeals-at-current-pace-remarks-calcutta-hc) that at the current pace, clearing them could take 21 years.

## Who is responsible

Under Article 324 of the Constitution, the voter list is the **Election Commission's** job. It ordered the SIR, set its rules, ran the flagging, and set up the tribunals.

The doubts aren't only from outside. In September, the Indian Express reported that the Commission's two other members, Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi, had [formally objected at least 14 times](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/why-two-election-commissioners-objected-to-sir-exercises-under-gyanesh-kumar) in 10 months, including over changes to the voter forms and how deletions, restorations and appeals were handled. The ECI [says](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/amp/nation/current-affairs/all-decisions-taken-in-unanimity-eci-denies-rift-over-sir-1989814) all its decisions were lawful and unanimous.

## The people who protested

That report set off protests across India from late September, led by young people under the banner of the Cockroach Janta Party, along with students, farmers' unions and opposition parties. They are asking Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar to resign and for the SIR to be reviewed.

The response has been to stop them gathering, in the name of keeping the peace:

- Before the 10 October rally at Jantar Mantar, Delhi Police [refused permission](https://thewire.in/government/delhi-police-deny-permission-to-oct-10-rally-order-closure-of-57-metro-stations), sought the closure of **57 metro stations**, and around 23,000 central personnel were sent to the city.
- On the day, **thousands were detained** in Delhi. Estimates range from about 2,000 to over 5,000 ([summary of reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_anti-CEC_protests)). About 150 more were detained in Chennai.
- Jantar Mantar was then taken off the list of official protest sites, and two of the movement's leaders were [sent to judicial custody for five days](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/top-indian-cockroach-leaders-jail-trespassing-jantar-mantar-saurav-das-ashutosh-ranka) on trespassing charges.
- On 23 October, a march to the ECI headquarters was stopped and [several protesters detained](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/india/several-protesters-detained-as-anti-sir-march-heads-towards-eci-headquarters-section-163-enforced-2026-10-23-1057748), under Section 163 orders banning gatherings.

The government says peaceful assembly is protected, subject to lawful restrictions, and that UN concerns about the crackdown are misplaced. The BJP calls the protests politically motivated.

## What I think

The SIR wasn't one big fraud, and I was wrong to assume that at first. Most removals were routine, the judges were not the problem, and the statewide result doesn't hinge on the deletions.

But it failed people in the places that matter. A software check decided who had to prove they belong, and it fell far harder on Muslims. Lakhs were removed with no hearing at all. And the fix arrives after the vote, if it arrives at all.

When the people asking the Election Commission to explain this are met with closed metro stations and police vans, that isn't peace. It's just quiet. Asking for accountability shouldn't be treated as a threat to public order.
